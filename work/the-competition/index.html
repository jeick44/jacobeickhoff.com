Engagement 03 · Four-person team · hedge fund case competition

The Competition

Four of us built a pipeline that read public filings and earnings transcripts to score how far each company in the S&P 500 had actually put AI into its operations, sorted the index on that score, and submitted a paper. “Operational AI Adoption in a Winner-Take-Most Economy.” On our own numbers, the top quartile beat the bottom against an equal-weight benchmark over the eight weeks to late February 2026. Run over the same stretch of each of the two years before that, the same signal pointed the wrong way.

Self-Computed Every performance figure from this Engagement was computed by the team from terminal data. Nobody outside the team checked it, and no independent party reviewed it. That is why What Changed reports the direction of all three periods and withholds their magnitudes until they are re-derived, rather than restating them from the paper.

What this Engagement produced

  • 1 paper submitted

    One formal submission, co-written by a team of four, on operational AI adoption across the index.

  • 3 measured periods

    Windows the signal was tested over. It pointed the right way in one of them.

  • 2 corpora read

    Public filings and earnings transcripts, machine-read into one adoption score per company.

Section 01

The Situation

A hedge fund case competition wants one thing: a formal paper carrying an investable idea and enough evidence that somebody can argue with it. Early in 2026 nearly every such idea priced who sells AI — chips, models, the clouds they run on. Far fewer were about who had actually put it to work.

That gap stays open because the evidence is awkward: companies say a great deal about AI in their filings and on their earnings calls, and most of it is positioning. So the question we took was whether the documents alone separate a company running AI in its operations from a company with an AI paragraph — and whether the market pays for the difference.

Why the paper was about operators rather than sellers

In a winner-take-most market the returns to a technology concentrate in a handful of firms, and the vendors are where that concentration shows first — their revenue is the adoption. For an operator the same adoption arrives as cost that has stopped being incurred, which surfaces later and through a noisier channel.

The paper’s argument is that the lag is where a public-market reader can still act: by the time an operator’s advantage is legible in its margin it is legible to everybody, and the disclosure documents move first. Whether that argument survives is a separate question from whether it was worth testing, and What Changed is about which of the two this turned out to be.

Section 02

What I Did

The team built a document-processing pipeline: pull the filings and the earnings-call transcripts for the index, split them into passages, score each company on the language of deployment rather than on the frequency of the word, roll the passages up to one score per company, cut the index into quartiles on it, and measure the top-quartile-minus-bottom spread forward against an equal-weight benchmark.

My own part, stated plainly: I contributed to the pipeline, read every prior year’s winning entry to work out what the judges actually rewarded, and co-wrote the final submission.

The other three owned the financial modelling, the index construction, and the presentation the paper was defended with. They are credited by what they owned rather than by name, for the same reason the Sponsor of Engagements 01 and 02 is not named: none of them has approved a public write-up of their work, and putting a co-author’s name on my portfolio is their decision to make, not mine.

Reading the prior winners before writing a word

The entries that had won in previous years were not the ones with the strongest backtest. They were the ones whose thesis could be stated in a sentence, and whose evidence a judge could check inside the time a judge actually has.

That changed what we produced: fewer exhibits, each defensible on its own, and every figure in the abstract carrying the window it was measured over. It is the most transferable thing I did here, and it was not analysis — it was finding out what the audience rewards before spending six weeks producing for it.

Counting mentions is not measuring adoption, and this is the part I would attack first

A filing that says “AI” forty times is a filing with a communications strategy. The score keys instead on the language of something actually running — named internal systems, capital expenditure attached to them, described changes to a process, headcount discussed in relation to them — and treats bare frequency as noise.

The weakness sits in the same place as the idea. The label is inferred from what a company chose to write down and is never checked against what it runs: a firm with a disciplined investor-relations team and no deployment scores like an operator, and a firm that deploys quietly does not score at all. We said so in the paper, and it is still the first thing I would go after.

Section 03

What Changed

Measured, and mine to state: one paper submitted, and the signal tested over three periods instead of the one that flattered it.

The result, by direction. Out of sample, over roughly eight weeks ending in late February 2026, the top quartile beat the bottom quartile against an equal-weight benchmark — a spread the team computed itself from terminal data. Run over the equivalent stretch of each of the two preceding years, the same signal produced a negative spread both times.

Withheld The magnitudes of the three spreads, and their standard errors, were measured — they are in the submitted paper — and they are not reproduced here. I will not restate a performance figure from memory on a page with my name on it, and I have not yet re-derived these from the source. That is a different thing from a Pending Result, which is an outcome nobody has measured yet; this one is measured and withheld until I have checked it. The paragraph gets replaced by the three figures at that point.

Two of the three periods went against us, and that is the finding rather than a footnote on it. An eight-week window that works, sitting on top of two years that do not, is far more likely to be an eight-week window than a signal — and a reader shown only the window that worked has been handed the one period out of three in which the thesis is true. All three went into the paper and all three are here.

For anyone reading Engagement 04 next: this scoring work and the pipeline I later withdrew in The Research are independent pieces of work, on the evidence of the two things I can actually check. They measure different constructs — operational adoption here, machine authorship there — over different corpora, and this paper was written months before that pipeline existed. I have not sat down and diffed the two, so that is the basis for the claim rather than more than it is; what it supports is that the withdrawal there does not reach back into the result here.

Section 04

What I Learned

That one good window is a window, and that the argument I had to make on my own team was for publishing the two that were not.

The case against reporting the inversion, and why it lost

The case against was real and not lazy: a competition rewards a clean result, the prior periods were out of sample in a direction nobody had asked about, and volunteering them invites a judge to spend his limited attention on our weakest exhibit.

What beat it: a judge who finds the inverted periods himself has found something, and a judge who is handed them is reading a paper that already knows where it is weak. The second is the better paper even when it scores worse. The inversion is also information — a signal that works only in the most recent window is a signal fitted to that window, whether or not anybody fitted it on purpose, and three eight-week stretches cannot tell those two apart.

The second thing is smaller and I keep relearning it: attribution on a team has to be written down while the work is happening. Six months later, working out who owned which part of a pipeline is reconstruction, and reconstruction flatters whoever is doing it. The single sentence about my own contribution in What I Did is narrower than what I could have claimed, because narrow and checkable is the only version worth anything in an interview.

Section 05

Where Else This Applies

Reporting the periods that went against you. Any performance claim — a strategy, a pricing change, a marketing channel, a process redesign — is measured over some window, and the window is usually chosen after the result is known. Stating the periods that failed alongside the one that worked is the only version a reader can price.

Measuring the behaviour rather than the disclosure. Reading an organisation from its own documents is the same problem in sustainability reporting, in risk, and in any diligence read of a target: the document says what somebody chose to write, and the gap between that and what it does is where the wrong conclusions come from.

Finding out what the audience rewards before producing for it. Prior winning entries, prior approved deals, prior board memos that got a decision — the record of what an audience has actually rewarded exists in most organisations and is almost never read before the work starts.
