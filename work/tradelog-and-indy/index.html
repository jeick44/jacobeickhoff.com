Engagement 05 · Self-directed

TradeLog and Indy

Every trading journal ends the session by asking whether you followed your rules, and then lets you answer. TradeLog answers from the broker’s own export instead — Verified Adherence, computed from the record of what was actually done — and keeps the trader’s own answer beside it, because the distance between the two is a signal the product refuses to average away. Indy is the charting toolkit that feeds it, written up here as the diagnosis it started as.

Design, not dollars The trader this was built for is me, which constrains what the page can say rather than licensing it to say more. No dollar figure, account balance, return or behavioural finding from that record appears here, and neither does any of the method the tooling was built around. What is published is the product and the architecture, which is the part that is mine to publish.

Section 01

The Situation

A trading journal asks whether the rules were followed and then accepts the answer of the person whose rules they were — typed in afterwards, about decisions made under pressure and reconsidered since. The entries most worth counting are the impulsive ones, taken without a plan and disowned on review, and those are the ones least likely to be written down at all.

So the figure a journal reports measures intention, and it improves with effort: the more carefully somebody journals, the more convincing a number they produce about the sessions the journal never saw.

The record that would settle it already exists. The broker writes down every execution as it happens — entry, exit, size, fees, timestamps — and hands over the file. Nothing needs remembering. It needs reading, and then something has to be willing to disagree with the trader about it.

Section 02

What I Did

TradeLog computes Verified Adherence from that export: Rules the trader committed to in advance, written as conditions a machine can check, evaluated against every execution the broker recorded. Nothing the trader types reaches it.

The simpler product deletes the self-report at that point, and this one does the opposite. Claimed Adherence — the trader’s own account of the same session — is kept beside the computed figure, and the difference is displayed and treated as a signal rather than reconciled away. The record knows what happened and can never know why; the trader knows why and has the only reason to be generous. That gap measures where belief has drifted from behaviour, which is what the journal was always supposed to be about, and averaging the two is the one operation that destroys it.

Three decisions hold that figure up.

The evaluator is deterministic, and the clock is an argument. One pure function takes the executions, the ruleset, the account, the annotations and now, and returns every derived number on every screen. It reads no clock and touches no storage. Injecting the time rather than reading it is what makes a session from March re-evaluate today to what it evaluated to in March, and nothing recomputes adherence on its own, so there is one answer rather than one per surface.

Rules are stored, versioned configuration. They have to be editable, or they are the app’s rules rather than the trader’s. But a threshold edited in place rewrites its own history: last month’s adherence silently becomes a claim about this month’s rule. So an edit mints a version and a day records the version it was graded under. A rule changed today changes what tomorrow is graded by and nothing already graded. The journaling rubric is versioned on the same seam one grain finer: Rules bind a day, because a clean day is a day-shaped claim, while a rubric binds the single entry it scored.

Order placement is rejected outright. The appeal was real: the app knows the budget is spent, and it could refuse the next order at the gateway rather than merely objecting to it. It was rejected because a gateway enforces only if it cannot be bypassed, and the broker’s own platform is one keystroke away — so it buys no enforcement while adding two failure modes that did not exist before, a bug that moves money and a failed request that leaves a position open. What is left reads and never writes, server-side, with the key held outside the browser. When the connection fails the app says so loudly: a stale count shown as a current one is worse than no count, because it gets believed.

Section 03

What Changed

The journal stopped being a place to type. Executions arrive by import and are the record; journaling became annotation of rows that already exist, restricted to what the broker cannot know. The headline figure changed with it: it had been a score the journal computed about its own journaling, which measures how completely a form was filled in, and it became the one that says how much room the account has left before it ends.

Indy is the other half of that record. It is a charting toolkit whose job is to remove drafting labour rather than to supply judgement: where it could present evidence or resolve it, it presents, and it carries no direction readout at all. What it adds here is an export — what a chart showed at a point in time, emitted as structured data and imported through the journal’s existing surface as an Observation, carrying no claim that a trade was placed. A week’s review became reading records instead of opening dated folders of screenshots.

It began as an inherited script that did a fraction of what I wanted and did that fraction wrong, and the first work was not the rewrite. It was saying exactly how it failed. One readout had never displayed once in months of use: a four-digit time was being handed to an argument expecting an hour, so every comparison fell through to the same branch. It was deleted rather than repaired — it existed to make the script look elaborate, nothing depended on it, and a requirement nobody has is not a requirement. A second defect had two possible behaviours and nothing to tell them apart: a daily reset keyed off a call given a timezone where it wanted a session, so either the overnight levels were cleared before the part of the day I actually traded, or they were never cleared at all. Reading the code did not decide which, and neither had using it. Naming both turned a vague intention to rebuild into a specification of fifty numbered requirements.

One decision there constrains the architecture permanently. The charting platform’s shared-library mechanism publishes source, to anyone, without exception — so the shared logic is not a library. It lives as modules in a private repository and a build step concatenates them into the three deployable scripts: one source of truth, three outputs, nothing published.

Withheld What the committed Rules would have done across my own history is computed exactly, from executions that already happened, and it is not on this page. That figure is a personal profit-and-loss number, and a number describing my trading account is not evidence about my engineering — it is what a reader would remember instead of it. The site’s own check now refuses the shape of those figures in any shipped file, so this is a property of what gets published rather than a promise about my restraint.

Section 04

What I Learned

The interventions were written from a story about the user rather than from the user’s record. Each one was plausible, and that was the whole of the evidence behind it. When the same import that produces Verified Adherence was finally pointed at the trigger list, one trigger turned out to be watching for a pattern the record did not contain. It was retired rather than kept on the grounds that it was plausible.

The uncomfortable part is that the check cost nothing and had been there from the first day the data was. A feature justified by a plausible story about a user is a feature nobody has checked, and I had built the instrument that could check it before it occurred to me to point it at my own product. Indy taught the same thing from the other end: a readout dead from the day it was written, on my chart every session for months, unnoticed precisely because nothing depended on it. “I use it and it works” is not evidence that it works.

The evidence this product would need, and why it is not here

TradeLog’s claim is behavioural: that computing compliance rather than collecting it changes what the user does. The only evidence that would settle it is a long enough run of sessions read against the same account before the change — a personal ledger, and the one document this page will not publish. Nothing here should be read as a claim that the product worked.

What the page claims is narrower and checkable: the figure is derived from a record the user does not author, the derivation reproduces on old data, and the decisions behind both were written down with their alternatives at the time rather than reconstructed afterwards for a write-up.

Section 05

Where Else This Applies

Self-report measures intention, and it fails hardest where it matters most. Control attestations, training completion, time recording, safety checklists — each asks the person who did the work to confirm they did it properly, and each is least reliable in exactly the cases worth catching. Where a system already holds a record of the behaviour, compliance can be computed from it, and the distance between claim and record is a second measurement most organisations throw away by reconciling it.

A threshold edited in place rewrites its own history. Credit policy, risk limits, eligibility criteria, service-level definitions: change one and last quarter’s compliance rate silently becomes a statement about this quarter’s rule, with nobody told. Versioning the rule and recording which version graded each case costs almost nothing at design time and cannot be retrofitted afterwards.

Refusing a capability is an architecture decision. The integration is not read-only because writing was hard. It is read-only because the write path added risk to a system whose whole purpose was to reduce it — and a decision recorded with the alternative it was chosen over survives the next person to notice the gap, including when that person is me, six months later, with a plausible reason.
