Six Engagements, each written the same way.

Every Engagement had a Sponsor, a scope and an outcome that was not mine to choose. Each one is written as a Case Study following the same five sections in the same order — The Situation, What I Did, What Changed, What I Learned, Where Else This Applies — so that two of them can be read against each other, and so that What I Learned reads as a method rather than an excuse.

  • Engagement 01

    Customer Intelligence

    A community bank in the Upper Midwest

    Browser-based mapping and profitability tools that gave leadership a spatial and demographic view of a customer base it had only ever seen as rows.

    On the map: Tools under someone else’s rules

    Read the Case Study

  • Engagement 02

    Credit Process Rebuild

    A community bank in the Upper Midwest · ongoing

    Loan origination rebuilt as local-only browser tools: intake, tax-document extraction, spreads, and an approval presentation restructured into three separate stages.

    On the map: Tools under someone else’s rules

    Read the Case Study

  • Engagement 03

    Hedge Fund Case Competition

    Four-person team

    Our paper, “Operational AI Adoption in a Winner-Take-Most Economy,” scored companies on how they describe putting AI to work, and reports the two earlier periods when the same signal pointed the wrong way.

    On the map: Analysis to a brief

    Read the Case Study

  • Engagement 04

    Research

    Independent · faculty-advised

    Tests whether AI editing can be spotted at three strengths, light, moderate and heavy, before any detection method is allowed near a real filing and its returns.

    On the map: Research I can be wrong in

    Read the Case Study

  • Engagement 05

    Investment Decision Tools

    Self-directed · product design and architecture

    Reads the broker’s record and never places an order. What the investor did sits beside what they said they did, never averaged, because the gap between the two is the signal. A companion charting tool records what each chart showed.

    On the map: Systems I own end to end

    Read the Case Study

  • Engagement 06

    Regime & Sentiment Allocator

    Self-directed

    Sorts the market into one of four regimes, allocates across fifteen sector and credit funds, and writes itself a plain-English briefing on a schedule. Fifty-six trading days is too short to judge returns, so it is published as a design.

    On the map: Systems I own end to end

    Read the Case Study
